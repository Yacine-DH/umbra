[1] arXiv:2611.14583 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Circuit-aware protection against burst errors in lattice surgery
Jaebum Kim
Comments: 8 pages, 5 figures
Subjects: Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

Large-scale quantum computing will require logical operations protected against high-energy impacts that disturb many superconducting qubits at once. Gap engineering suppresses relaxation induced by these impacts, but protecting only a subset of qubits requires choosing where that protection is placed. During lattice surgery, measurements change as patches merge and split, exposing different ancillas and temporal boundaries to burst errors. Here we compare three static protection layouts for a simulated lattice-surgery CNOT, matching the numbers of protected ancillas and the X:Z check-type splits. At distance 9, protecting the check type measured during each merge reduces burst-induced excess risk by $38^{+6}_{-5}\%$ relative to a deep geometric band under correlated background matching, without giving the decoder the impact parameters. We attribute this difference to report errors at temporal boundaries and ancilla faults propagating to data, which interact to increase the excess risk of a shallow band. We also find that the typed layout reaches the shallow-band reference risk with an estimated $49^{+2}_{-14}$ fewer protected ancillas. At distance 13, the advantage is not resolved under oracle decoding, and a five-round burst cap reverses the shallow-band ordering. These results indicate how the measurements of a logical circuit can guide protection placement within the simulated decoder and burst-duration regimes tested here.

[2] arXiv:2611.14588 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Controlled preparation and unconditional transport on a fluctuating quantum graph
Xianbin Wang
Comments: 13 pages, 1 figure; includes appendices. Reproducibility code and data provided as ancillary files
Subjects: Quantum Physics (quant-ph); Mathematical Physics (math-ph)

Lieb--Robinson bounds limit the spread of quantum information, but do not establish that a prescribed input can be prepared and transmitted with appreciable probability. For quantum graphs with fluctuating edges, connecting spectral propagation to unconditional readout also requires control of graph coherence, finite boundaries, and preparation errors. We address this problem in a one-probe model on a prescribed square candidate lattice, combining a generalized Rokhsar--Kivelson parent, spectral reduction, adiabatic preparation, and local dynamical estimates. Assuming a supplied coherent graph ground state, we construct a finite-source wave packet and a hopping ramp that populate an isolated branch with strictly positive group velocity. We prove an unconditional arrival probability of at least $5/8$ in a specified receiver region of a sufficiently large finite open square. Explicit bounds on local joint-state and Hamiltonian errors retain a lower probability of $9/16$, without requiring global many-body state closeness or postselection on graph measurements. Exact arithmetic certificates and a separate small-system numerical diagnostic support the quantitative analysis. The construction provides a model-specific benchmark for transport and readout in fluctuating quantum networks, linking a propagation mechanism to an observable lower bound and identifying the local error budgets required to preserve it. It distinguishes suppression of unwanted influence from guaranteed signal delivery, a useful separation for quantum-network and emergent-geometry models. The resource estimates are conservative, graph-state supply is assumed, and the result does not establish background-free geometry or gravitational dynamics.

[3] arXiv:2611.14611 [pdf, html, other]
Title: Probing Krylov Dynamics in Driven Many-Body Systems
András Grabarits, Adolfo del Campo
Comments: 17 pages of main text, 16 pages of appendices, 6 figures
Subjects: Quantum Physics (quant-ph); Quantum Gases (cond-mat.quant-gas)

Krylov complexity has become a central probe of operator growth, scrambling, and quantum chaos. Yet, a basic fact hinders its experimental study: laboratory quantum systems are routinely driven in time, whereas the Krylov construction assumes a fixed generator and breaks down once the Hamiltonian varies. Here, we solve the time-dependent many-body Krylov problem exactly and in closed form for scale- and rotation-invariant systems realized in current quantum-simulation and control platforms. We find exact relations that map the driven dynamics onto mutually orthogonal Krylov subspaces and yield a closed-form spread complexity, determined solely by the spectral properties of the initial Hamiltonian and the driving schedule. The construction extends to Liouville space and solves the time-dependent density-matrix Krylov problem, which makes the Krylov dynamics of initial thermal states experimentally accessible. In scale-invariant quantum gases, such as the driven Lieb-Liniger model, the Tonks-Girardeau gas, and the Calogero-Sutherland model, the complexity is set by the width of the atomic cloud and its rate of change, directly accessible via in situ and time-of-flight imaging. In rotation-invariant spin systems such as the Haldane-Shastry and central-spin models, it is governed by collective spin precession that can be probed and read out in trapped-ion, cavity-QED, and circuit-QED platforms.

[4] arXiv:2611.14622 [pdf, other]
Title: Squeezed Light Generation by Cooper Pair Recombination in a Superconductor-Semiconductor Structure
Sima Buchbinder, Avi Koriat, Alex Hayat
Journal-ref: Phys. Rev. B 114, 134506 (2026)
Subjects: Quantum Physics (quant-ph); Superconductivity (cond-mat.supr-con)

We theoretically demonstrate squeezed light generation in a compact, electrically driven device utilizing the nonlinear light-matter interaction of Cooper-pair recombination in a hybrid superconductorsemiconductor structure. On the basis of second-order perturbation theory, we developed an effective Hamiltonian which incorporates the electronic component of the interaction as a photon coupling strength coefficient, enabling a model to describe the quadrature squeezing effect of Cooper-pair recombination on the photonic state. We find the squeezing strength to be proportional to the superconducting energy gap squared and device length, as well as increasing with applied voltage. For practical device length, materials and voltage bias, we calculated squeezing values comparable to those of existing squeezed light generation methods when operating below the critical temperature. This theoretically developed concept shows viability for electrically pumped squeezed light generation and can pave the way towards realization of squeezing in compact integrated structures.

[121] arXiv:2611.14607 (cross-list from physics.atom-ph) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Optical trapping of neutral atoms via magnetic-dipole transitions
E. Orellana-Paillaleo, N. Vera, P. Solano
Comments: 10 pages, 5 figures
Subjects: Atomic Physics (physics.atom-ph); Optics (physics.optics); Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

Optical trapping of neutral atoms is almost universally described as an electric-dipole (E1) effect. We show that the magnetic field of light, acting on a magnetic-dipole (M1) transition, can induce an ac Zeeman shift significantly larger than the ac Stark shift, thereby enabling magnetic trapping. We construct the M1 polarizability tensor and identify a critical detuning $\delta_c$, independent of optical power, below which the magnetic interaction dominates. We analyze suitable trapping parameters and possible loss mechanisms, particularly for the intraconfigurational transitions $^3H_6\rightarrow{}^3H_5$ of erbium at $1437.13$~nm and $^5I_8\rightarrow{}^5I_7$ of dysprosium at $2418.84$~nm. We propose two trapping configurations: a hybrid magnetic/electric optical tweezer and an optical lattice with separated magnetic and electric antinodes, producing state-dependent purely magnetic and purely electric trapping wells. Our results present optical magnetic fields as a suitable resource to expand the toolbox for atomic manipulation.

[122] arXiv:2611.14629 (cross-list from hep-ph) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Quantum Computing Enhanced Search for Wavelike Dark Matter
Yunjia Bao, Yu Cheng, Liang Jiang, Lian-Tao Wang
Comments: 6 pages, 2 figures, plus Supplemental Material
Subjects: High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); High Energy Physics - Experiment (hep-ex); Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

Identifying a weak, stochastic signal hidden within a broad frequency band is a central challenge in the search for wavelike dark matter. We explore quantum computing enhanced sensing based on the Grover search algorithm to address this problem and find that a long integration time stabilizes the accumulated sensing phase, reducing quantum signal processing (QSP) errors induced by dark matter fluctuations. We further propose alternating the signs of the QSP angles between oracle calls to suppress the coherent accumulation of the intrinsic QSP approximation errors during the search. Combining these strategies in a transmon-based benchmark, we obtain a projected sensitivity to axion dark matter beyond existing astrophysical limits. This opens access to substantial unexplored parameter space across the $10$-$100\,\mu\mathrm{eV}$ mass range and offers an accelerated discovery channel for wavelike dark matter. With $10^6$ entangled sensing qubits, the projected sensitivity reaches the QCD axion band. Although illustrated with transmons, the protocol can be adapted to other experimental platforms for wavelike dark matter detection.

[123] arXiv:2611.14641 (cross-list from hep-ph) [pdf, html, other]
Title: A Grover-Accelerated Search for Dark Matter
Zachary Bogorad, Roni Harnik
Comments: 27 pages, 10 figures, 1 table
Subjects: High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph); High Energy Physics - Experiment (hep-ex); Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

We show that Grover's algorithm can be used to parametrically accelerate wave-like dark matter searches. Despite having a finite coherence time $\tau$, wavelike dark matter can imprint a coherent $O(1)$ phase onto a sensor through a dispersive coupling over an integration time which significantly exceeds $\tau$. As such, we show that dark matter can be used as a repeatable quantum phase gate in a quantum device. If the sensor is prepared in a superposition of frequencies, we show that this phase can be transferred to a qubit register and digitized. The qubits are thus available for quantum computational processing in which dark matter can take part as a coherent gate. We show that Grover's algorithm can be used to speed up a search for dark matter in $N_{\rm bins}$ frequency bins by a factor of $\sqrt{N_{\rm bins}}$. Although the technological challenges to implementing such a search remain substantial, this demonstrates that there is no fundamental obstruction to accelerating wave-like dark matter searches with quantum computational techniques over many coherence times.

[124] arXiv:2611.14797 (cross-list from cond-mat.dis-nn) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Zeroth persistent homology of ground-state correlations at clean and disordered quantum transitions
Francisco Belchí, Nahuel Statuto
Comments: 16 pages, 7 figures, 2 tables, plus 7 pages of Supplemental Material (4 figures, 3 tables). Companion paper: arXiv:2611.11358. Code and data: this https URL
Subjects: Disordered Systems and Neural Networks (cond-mat.dis-nn); Statistical Mechanics (cond-mat.stat-mech); Strongly Correlated Electrons (cond-mat.str-el); Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

The antiferromagnetic Ising chain in transverse and longitudinal fields exhibits a continuous critical point, a mixed-order transition line and, with quenched disorder, an infinite-disorder fixed point and reentrant order. Using exact free-fermion solutions and the density-matrix renormalization group, we examine what the zeroth persistent homology of staggered ground-state correlations adds to conventional responses. Its barcode records the merge scales of the single-linkage hierarchy, and the maximum of its persistence-landscape velocity defines a threshold-free pseudocritical point. This point locates the clean critical point and the mixed-order line with an accuracy comparable to conventional responses. In the reentrant regime, the global velocity misplaces the entry, which is recovered only by restricting the filtration to the ordering scale, and none of the tested estimators locates the exit. A domain wall forced by the boundary conditions suppresses the disorder-averaged reentrant lobe. For individual disordered realizations, the labelled merge tree recovers most strong-disorder renormalization-group clusters, including noncontiguous ones missed by lattice-restricted hierarchies, with increasing agreement at stronger disorder. This information resides in the full correlation matrix, while zeroth homology provides a stable, threshold-free hierarchy.

[163] arXiv:2108.08342 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Quantum Measurements and Conservation Laws
Edward J. Gillis
Comments: Modified title. Additional discussion and examples. References added. 11 pages
Subjects: Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

When a measurement is made on a system that is not in an eigenstate of the measured observable, it is often assumed that some conservation law has been violated. But this assumption results from ignoring the entanglement between the measured system and the preparation apparatus. The preparation of a system in any particular state necessarily involves interaction between the apparatus and the system, and this interaction generates entanglement. The amount of entanglement is very small, but the apparatus typically has an enormous number of degrees of freedom; this implies that a very tiny difference between the apparatus states that are correlated with the orthogonal states of the measured system can be sufficient to account for the perceived deviation from strict conservation of the quantity in question. After reviewing entanglement relations for a typical measurement situation with a macroscopic preparation apparatus I identify microscopic processes involving analogous entanglement relations in which measurements on both entangled systems demonstrate exact conservation of the relevant quantities. The close analogy strongly suggests that conservation laws hold exactly in all measurement processes.

[164] arXiv:2208.10336 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Factorization, partner Hamiltonians and intelligent states in $\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$-symmetric position dependent effective mass systems
Pinaki Patra
Subjects: Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

We study a factorized $\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$-symmetric position-dependent mass Hamiltonian on a finite interval with quasiperiodic boundary conditions. A positive, bounded, and boundedly invertible metric is constructed explicitly, and the Hamiltonian is shown to be self-adjoint in the associated metric Hilbert space and similar to a Hermitian position-dependent-mass operator. Consequently, its spectrum is real within the stated parameter domain. The reverse factorization defines a $\mathcal{P}\mathcal{T}$-symmetric partner Hamiltonian, and the first-order factors act as intertwining operators. Eigenstates of the lowering-type factorization operator are consequently interpreted as Robertson intelligent states, not as states generated by an energy ladder. Their admissible eigenvalues are quantized by the quasiperiodic boundary condition, and they saturate the corresponding Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation. A weakly position-dependent mass profile illustrates the construction within the explicitly quantified regime.

[165] arXiv:2210.13608 (replaced) [pdf, html, other]
Title: Quantum randomness certification with untrusted measurements and few probe states
Makoto Ishihara, Carles Roch i Carceller, Kieran Neil Wilkinson, Casper Ahl Breum, Tobias Gehring, Jonatan Bohr Brask
Journal-ref: Phys. Rev. A 114, 052413 (2026)
Subjects: Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

We present a scheme for semidevice-independent quantum randomness certification from an untrusted measurement device and a trusted source and demonstrate it experimentally. No assumptions about noise or imperfections in the measurement are required, and the scheme is simple to implement with existing technology. The measurement device is probed with a few trusted states, and the output entropy can be lower bounded conditioned on the observed outcome distribution. The protocol can be applied to measurements with any finite number of outcomes and, in particular, can be realized by homodyne measurements of the vacuum using a detector probed by coherent states, as we experimentally demonstrate by intensity modulation of a telecom-wavelength pilot laser followed by homodyne detection and discretization by analog-to-digital conversion. We show that randomness can be certified in the presence of both Gaussian additive noise and non-Gaussian imperfections.

[166] arXiv:2308.01708 (replaced) [pdf, other]
Title: Local entanglement of electrons in 1D hydrogen molecule
Ivan P. Christov
Journal-ref: Entropy 2023, 25, 1308
Subjects: Quantum Physics (quant-ph)

The quantum entanglement entropy of the electrons in one-dimensional hydrogen molecule is quantified locally using an appropriate partitioning of the two-dimensional configuration space. Both the global and the local entanglement entropy exhibit a monotonic increase when increasing the inter-nuclear distance, while the local entropy remains peaked at the middle between the nuclei with its width decreasing. Our findings show that at the inter-nuclear distance where stable hydrogen molecule is formed, the quantum entropy shows no peculiarity thus indicating that the entropy and the energy measures display different sensitivity with respect to the interaction between the two identical electrons involved. One possible explanation is that the calculation of the quantum entropy does not account explicitly for the distance between the nuclei, which contrasts to the total energy calculation where the energy minimum depends decisively on that distance. The numerically exact and the time-dependent quantum Monte Carlo calculations show close results.
